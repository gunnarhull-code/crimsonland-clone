## Question

Detail the exact numeric/behavioral parameters for the enemy AI state machine fixed in [06 - enemy AI shape](06-enemy-ai-shape.md):

- The aggro radius: an exact value (or formula relative to the 1280×720 arena from [02](02-arena-session-structure.md)), not just "large, ~half the screen."
- The wander re-roll interval: exact seconds (or a range), and whether it's fixed or randomized per-roll.
- For each of Spider, Rat, and Alien: the concrete definitions of their ~3 themed wander variants (what shape/pattern each variant actually traces, and each variant's speed), and their chase-style parameters once aggroed (Spider's "charge-stop-charge" is described in [06](06-enemy-ai-shape.md) and [05](05-enemy-roster.md) but not quantified; Rat and Alien's chase styles aren't detailed at all yet).
- How the "big/boss" variant's slower speed is derived (a flat multiplier? a fixed value per species?).
- Whether the soft arena-boundary drift for wandering enemies (from [02](02-arena-session-structure.md): "only a couple seconds" off-screen) has a specific max duration and how enemies re-enter (do they retain their pre-drift wander variant, or re-roll on return?).

## Answer

**Aggro radius**: flat 450px for all species.

**Wander re-roll interval**: randomized 1.0-2.0s per roll (not fixed) — keeps the "looking around" read organic rather than metronomic.

**Spider** — Wander Variants: *Loop* (small circle, ~40px radius), *Figure-eight* (~60px lobes), *Skitter-pause* (dart 30-50px in a random direction, then freeze ~0.5s); wander speed 60% of chase speed. Chase Style: charge-stop-charge — burst ~0.4s at high speed, pause ~0.3s, repeat.

**Rat** — Wander Variants: *Tight loop* (~20px radius, fast), *Zigzag dart* (quick alternating-direction darts), *Freeze-scurry* (short scurry, brief freeze, shorter than Spider's); wander speed 90% of chase speed (Rat barely changes gear — speed is its identity). Chase Style: straight relentless beeline at full constant speed, no stutter — the swarm-rush contrast to Spider.

**Alien** — Wander Variants: *Wide loop* (~100px radius, slow), *Long drift* (slow straight wander for a few seconds, then turns), *Idle sway* (minor side-to-side, nearly stationary); wander speed 40% of chase speed. Chase Style: steady constant-speed straight-line charge, no burst, no stutter — the brute contrast to both other species.

**Boss Variant speed**: a flat multiplier of 60% applied to whichever Wander Variant or Chase Style speed the base species is currently using — danger is telegraphed through size/HP (not yet defined, see gap below), not movement.

**Soft arena-boundary drift**: threshold is ~1.5s (within the "second or two" the user specified). Once a wandering enemy has been past the visible edge longer than that threshold, it stops pursuing its current Wander Variant's target point and instead steers — smoothly, blended with its existing velocity, not a teleport or snap — back toward a point inside the Arena, so it never reads as unnatural. Once back inside, it resumes its current Wander Variant rather than re-rolling (avoids a visual pop right on re-entry).

**Gap surfaced**: enemy HP, contact damage dealt to the player, and per-kill XP reward are not defined anywhere on this map yet — needed before Boss Variant "danger via size/HP" above can actually be implemented. New ticket opened: [15 - enemy combat stats](15-enemy-combat-stats.md).

**Addendum — Spider's charge-stop-charge randomized**: the fixed ~0.4s burst / ~0.3s pause above read as a metronome in actual play, not a jerky spider. Per direct playtest request, both numbers (and the burst's travel speed) are now randomized per cycle: burst 0.15-0.55s at 0.6-1.5× chase speed, pause 0.12-0.5s. Same randomization applied to the Skitter-pause Wander Variant's freeze length (was fixed ~0.5s, now the same 0.12-0.5s range) and its dart speed (0.6-1.5×), so the jerkiness is consistent whether the Spider is wandering or chasing. Rat and Alien's timings are untouched.

**Addendum — chase no longer perfect-tracks the player, across all species**: every species' chase beelined at the player's exact current position with the direction snapping instantly every frame - with several enemies aggroed at once, that reads as one tight clump perfectly mirroring the player's every move, not a spread-out crowd. Per direct playtest request ("I don't want them all following me perfectly... they need to be spread out more"), each aggroed enemy now:
- Aims at the player's position plus a personal offset (30-110px, random direction) instead of the exact position, so simultaneous chasers fan out around the player rather than converging on one point.
- Turns toward that target at a capped rate (70-1000°/s, randomized per enemy) instead of snapping - a low roll overshoots corners into a wide arc; a high roll reads as near-perfect tracking. "Some of them can, sometimes."

Both the offset and the turn-rate cap reroll every 2-4s per enemy (not fixed for its whole chase), so no enemy is locked into one flanking spot or one turn sharpness for the whole encounter. This sits entirely inside `_process_chase` - the aggro trigger, the 450px radius, and each species' base chase speed above are unchanged; only how directly each enemy steers toward the player changed.

**Addendum — auto-aggro timeout**: now that the Arena is much bigger than the screen ([02](02-arena-session-structure.md)'s addendum), an enemy that spawns far from the player could wander indefinitely without ever crossing the 450px aggro radius - it would just read as "standing there." Per direct playtest request, each enemy now also force-aggroes after a randomized 8-16s of wandering, independent of proximity. Rolled once per enemy at spawn, not a global timer, so a crowd doesn't all snap to aggro on the same frame. **Confirmed correct as-is** on a later playtest pass ("the enemies should auto-aggro you after a few seconds. I'm okay with them hanging around for a bit though") - no change made here, this addendum's behavior already matches what was asked for.

**Addendum — instant aggro on taking damage, and alerting neighbors**: per the same playtest pass - "if they get shot at, they should instant aggro... if they see somebody right next to them getting shot at... just go into aggro mode" (explicitly *not* "aggro toward the shooter" - just the plain on/off `aggroed` flag this ticket already has, not a per-attacker target). `Enemy.gd`'s `take_damage()` now sets `aggroed = true` on itself immediately, and also on every other enemy within a 180px `HIT_ALERT_RADIUS` - a wandering enemy no longer has to wait out the 450px proximity check or the 8-16s auto-aggro timeout if it (or a neighbor) is already under fire. Since `aggroed` is a plain bool rather than a reference to which player is attacking, this can't "flip-flop between aggros" in a hypothetical future multiplayer context (a concern raised in the same message) - there's no target to flip-flop between yet, only on/off.

**Addendum — "more natural" movement**: three changes, applied uniformly across all species and both wander/chase, requested together as "the monster movement needs to be a little bit more natural":
- **Acceleration instead of instant velocity**: every enemy's actual `velocity` now eases toward whatever its current behavior wants (a wander target, a chase direction, a burst/freeze toggle) at a capped 900px/s² via `Vector2.move_toward`, rather than snapping there in one frame. Smooths out the burst/freeze and aggro-transition instants from a hard stop/start into a real accelerate/decelerate.
- **Separation steering**: each enemy nudges away from other enemies within 46px, blended into its desired velocity rather than a hard push - purely cosmetic (no physics collision exists per [02](02-arena-session-structure.md)'s collision addendum), just discourages bodies from visibly sliding through each other in a crowd.
- **Per-enemy speed variance**: a fixed ×0.85-1.15 multiplier rolled once at spawn, applied to both wander and chase speed, so a same-species group doesn't move in perfect lockstep.

All three are cheap, general-purpose steering additions, not species-specific - if a particular species still reads as unnatural after playtesting this, that's the next thing to look at.

**Addendum — reaction lag, to stop kiting from re-clumping the crowd**: the chase-spread addendum above fixed simultaneous aggro reading as one tight clump, but a *kited* crowd (the player circling/weaving) still converged back into a tight group over time, because every enemy aimed at the player's exact live position - reported directly ("they all just target me... if I just kite them around, they end up just grouping up into a big, tight group... they don't just seem like robots"). Every enemy beyond `CHASE_CLOSE_RANGE` now aims at a point sampled from a new rolling history buffer on `Player.gd` (`get_position_delayed()`, ~1.2s of recent positions) instead of the player's live position - each enemy rolls its own reaction lag (0.1-0.8s, rerolled alongside the aim offset/turn rate every 2-4s) so they're each reacting to a slightly different moment of the player's recent path. This directly breaks the re-convergence during kiting (different enemies are chasing different points along the trail, not the same instantaneous target) and reads as personality - some enemies react almost instantly, others visibly lag - rather than every enemy uniformly, telepathically tracking the player's current position.

**Addendum — across-the-board speed reduction**: per direct playtest request ("a lot of the enemies are too fast and I am too fast as well... slow down a little bit except for the bullets"), every species' `chase_speed_px_s` and `wander_speed_px_s` in [enemies.csv](../enemies.csv) is now ×0.8 of the values above (e.g. Rat 160/144 → 128/115), and the Player's move speed (`Player.gd`'s `BASE_MOVE_SPEED`, not previously recorded on any ticket) is 260 → 210, also roughly ×0.8. Projectile speeds ([11](11-weapon-stat-table.md)) are explicitly untouched, per the request. A flat, uniform multiplier - relative pacing between species (Spider fastest, Alien slowest) is unchanged, the whole game just runs at ~80% of its prior pace.

**Addendum — bugfix: enemies weren't actually dangerous**: reported directly ("I was standing in the middle of a bunch of mobs and they just sort of barely did damage... it took forever to die... I want them to consistently do damage"). Two compounding causes, both fixed:
1. `Enemy.gd`'s `_process_contact_damage()` only decremented the attack cooldown timer *while touching* - so an enemy that bounced in and out of contact range (a side effect of this ticket's own turn-rate-limiting and separation-steering additions above) had its cooldown clock effectively pause every time contact broke, landing far fewer hits than `attack_cooldown_sec` implies. The cooldown now always ticks down in real time regardless of touching; touching only gates whether a *ready* attack can land. Verified via temporary instrumentation: the same test scenario went from 1 hit in 2 seconds to 2 hits in 2 seconds (Rat's 0.6s cooldown) after the fix.
2. The chase-aim-offset addition above (fanning enemies out at range) was also keeping them orbiting *just past* contact distance once they arrived, rather than ever actually closing in - so even a stationary-relative-to-player enemy might rarely register as touching at all. Fixed: within 100px of the player, an enemy now aims straight at the player's real position instead of its offset point - the offset still does its job at range, it just no longer overrides the final approach.

Separately, and *not* a bug: the Player has a genuine 0.5s invulnerability window after choosing a level-up perk ([14](14-player-vitals-and-leveling.md)) - the user flagged this themselves as a possible confound during the session that prompted this fix, and it's worth stating plainly here as the one case where "touching but no damage" is intentional.

**Addendum — species wander personality: Rat packs, Spider chills**: per direct playtest request ("I want the spiders to sort of just chill around. But maybe the rats can just move around as a group... meander"), Rat and Spider's *wander*-phase behavior (not chase - both keep their existing chase styles from [06](06-enemy-ai-shape.md)) diverged from the shared loop/dart repertoire above:
- **Rat** now wanders exclusively via a new `group_meander` variant (replacing its old solo tight_loop/zigzag_dart/freeze_scurry rotation): each Rat steers toward the average position of other Rats within 150px (simple decentralized flocking cohesion, no leader/follower state) plus a slow shared drift direction. The drift angle is a pure function of wall-clock time (`sin(time * 0.1) * TAU`), so every Rat computes the *identical* direction independently with zero inter-agent messaging - that's what makes a pack drift together rather than each Rat wandering off on its own.
- **Spider**'s wander pool is now `["skitter_pause", "skitter_pause", "loop"]` (duplicated to bias the reroll odds without a full weighted-random system) and its `wander_speed_px_s` dropped from 106/63 (base/boss) to 45/27 in [enemies.csv](../enemies.csv) - mostly still, with an occasional short dart, rather than continuously looping. Chase speed (176/106) is untouched, so a Spider is still dangerous once aggroed - only its idle behavior got calmer.
